Personalised explanation

Same concept.
Four ways in.

Not every student understands the same idea the same way. TEAiCH notices which explanations work for each learner and generates the next one in that style, with visuals, patterns, stories or algebra, all from the same textbook item.

A gardener plants 23 roses in the first row.

Every new row, she plants 2 fewer than the row before.

She stops when a row has only 5. So she has dropped from 23 to 5: that is 18 roses' worth of "fewer".

18 ÷ 2 = 9 times she took two away. Nine steps after the first row…

…means 10 rows in the bed.

aₙ = a + (n − 1)d the nth term
5 = 23 + (n − 1)(−2) substitute a = 23, d = −2, aₙ = 5
−18 = (n − 1)(−2) subtract 23
9 = n − 1 divide by −2
n = 10 rows

    Tutor rules for this chapter

    ↑ These rules drive the whiteboard demo above. Set 1 hint and "Never", then try a wrong answer.
    Responsible by design

    Built to respect
    teachers and schools.

    1
    Only from the textbook

    Answers are built from checked curriculum items and cite them. Outside the syllabus, the tutor says so.

    2
    Hints before answers

    It guides with questions and steps. A full solution opens only when the teacher's rules allow.

    3
    Checked before shown

    Calculations are verified by code and every statement traced to the book; anything that fails is not shown.

    4
    The teacher is in the loop

    Teachers see how each student uses the tutor and decide what it may do for that student.

    5
    Minimal, private data

    Notes stay on the device. No more personal data than learning needs, with parental consent.

    6
    Learning to learn with AI

    Students practise asking good questions and checking answers, a skill that outlasts the app.
